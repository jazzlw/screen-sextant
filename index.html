Optional — gives your distance from the screen.

Method. Rectilinear pinhole model. f_px = max(W,H) × f₃₅ / 36, then θ = 2·atan(w_px / 2·f_px) for the head-on figure and a difference of arctangents for the as-seen one. Arctangents are differenced rather than scaled, so off-axis targets don't inflate. Distance is exact similar triangles, D = W × f_px / w_px. The screen is fitted as a minimum-area rectangle over the lit region's convex hull, then refined to a quadrilateral, so w_px is measured along the screen's own axis rather than the image's.

Keystone. Off the centreline a screen images as a trapezoid, and the fronto-parallel shortcut above stops holding. With f_px known, two vanishing points give the 3D directions of the screen's edges, their cross product gives the plane normal, and back-projecting each corner ray onto that plane recovers the true rectangle up to one overall scale — which is all the angles need. At 20° off the centreline, ignoring this reads the aspect ratio 13% low.

Error budget. EXIF reports a rounded nominal focal length, so expect 1–2% — photograph a known length at a laser-ranged distance, back out f_px, type the implied f₃₅ here and save it for the camera. Camera roll and keystone are both fitted rather than assumed away, so neither a hand-held tilt nor an off-centreline seat inflates the reading. Don't pinch-zoom, though: digital crop is silent unless the camera tags it. And if the file carries no focal length, nothing is reported until you supply one — every angle scales directly with it.

What it detects. The projected image, not the physical screen. With masking closed on a scope feature you'll get 2.39:1 and that's the honest answer for what you're watching.
