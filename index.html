Optional — gives your distance from the screen.

Method. Rectilinear pinhole model. f_px = max(W,H) × f₃₅ / 36, then θ = 2·atan(w_px / 2·f_px) for the head-on figure and a difference of arctangents for the as-seen one. Arctangents are differenced rather than scaled, so off-axis targets don't inflate. Distance is exact similar triangles, D = W × f_px / w_px. The screen is fitted as a minimum-area rectangle over the lit region's convex hull, so w_px is measured along the screen's own axis rather than the image's.

Error budget. EXIF reports a rounded nominal focal length, so expect 1–2% — photograph a known length at a laser-ranged distance, back out f_px, type the implied f₃₅ here and save it for the camera. Camera roll is fitted rather than assumed away — the detector returns a rotated rectangle, so a hand-held tilt no longer inflates the reading. Don't pinch-zoom, though: digital crop is silent unless the camera tags it.

What it detects. The projected image, not the physical screen. With masking closed on a scope feature you'll get 2.39:1 and that's the honest answer for what you're watching.
